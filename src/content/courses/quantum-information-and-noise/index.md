This reading course is written for researchers who will work on quantum networks: how much a noisy fibre can carry, and how the nodes of a network should decide what to do. It starts from one particle of light and builds up to noise channels, information measures, capacity bounds and decisions in a network.

## How every unit works

Each unit has two parts:

- **Part A: one idea at a time.** Fifteen short sections. Every section asks a question in plain words, answers it first with an everyday picture (sunglasses, shadows, clocks, waves, gloves, rain on a roof), then gives the quantum version and the symbols physicists use, and ends with a box saying exactly where the everyday picture breaks.
- **Part B: exercises, solutions and reading.** Exercises in the same order as Part A, full solutions, a short external reading list, a glossary and a symbol dictionary.

Grey *Maths refresher* boxes re-teach each piece of mathematics the first time it is needed, and *What it represents* and *Reading the symbols* boxes say what each new symbol stands for and how to read it aloud.

Every unit comes as a slide deck and as detailed notes. The slides follow the same order and give the shape of each argument; the notes give every step. Each slide's footer says which section of the notes it belongs to.

## What you need before starting

Only school mathematics: arithmetic, a little trigonometry (sine and cosine), and squaring. Everything else is re-taught at the point where it is first used.
